Know Your Dyes

The Dye Category Guide

Understanding the right dye for your fabric and application

🟢
VAT Dyes
Cellulosic · Cotton · Linen
VAT dyes are insoluble pigments reduced to a soluble form for dyeing, then oxidised back on the fibre. They deliver exceptional wash fastness, light fastness, and are the gold standard for deep, long-lasting shades on cotton and cellulosic fabrics.
High Wash Fastness Light Stable Deep Shades Cotton
🟠
Naphthol Dyes
Azoic · Cotton · Bold Shades
Naphthol (azoic) dyes are a two-component system — a naphthol base coupling with a diazonium salt on the fibre to produce intense, bold colours. Ideal for brilliant reds, oranges, blues, and khakis with excellent dry-cleaning fastness.
Bold Colours Azoic Chemistry Dry-Clean Safe Two-Part System
🔵
Reactive Dyes
Cotton · Viscose · Silk
Reactive dyes form a covalent bond with the fibre, becoming part of the fabric itself. This makes them the most wash-fast option for brilliant, vibrant shades. Perfect for fashion textiles, home furnishings, and prints where colour clarity matters most.
Vibrant Shades Covalent Bond Best Wash-Fast Silk / Viscose
🧪
Base Dyes
Coupling · Azoic · Cotton
Base dyes are the coupling component in the azoic (Naphthol) dyeing system. Applied first to the fabric, they react with a diazonium salt on the fibre surface to develop bold, insoluble azo shades. Examples include Red GL Base and Blue B Base — essential for producing vivid, deep colours with excellent fastness on cotton.
Coupling Agent Azoic System Bold Shades Cotton

Industry Knowledge

Did You Know?

Useful facts about textile dyes every buyer should know

01
VAT Dyes are the Oldest Industrial Dye
VAT dyes, including natural indigo, have been used for thousands of years. Modern synthetic VAT dyes were developed in the early 1900s and remain the benchmark for durability on cotton and cellulosic fibres.
02
Naphthol Dyes Need Two Steps
Unlike most dyes, Naphthol (azoic) dyes are created directly on the fabric in two stages — the base is applied first, then coupled with a diazonium salt to produce the final colour. This is what makes them so bold and insoluble.
03
Reactive Dyes Bond with the Fibre
Reactive dyes form a covalent chemical bond with the fabric molecule itself — not just a surface coating. This is why they deliver the highest wash fastness and are the go-to choice for fashion-grade textiles.
04
"M.F" Means Machine Formulated
Products labelled M.F (Machine Formulated) are pre-standardised blends engineered for automated dyeing lines. They reduce manual adjustment, ensure colour consistency batch to batch, and save time on the production floor.
05
Base Dyes Are the First Half of Azoic Dyeing
Base dyes like Red GL Base and Blue B Base are not used alone — they are the coupling component in the azoic system. The vivid final colour only develops when the base reacts with a diazonium salt on the cotton fibre surface.
06
Wash Fastness is Rated 1–5
The textile industry rates colour fastness on a scale of 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent). VAT and Reactive dyes typically score 4–5 for wash fastness, making them the preferred choice for garments and home furnishings that are washed frequently.

Do you supply across India? +
Yes. Moksh Dyes & Chemicals supplies to textile mills, fabric manufacturers, and processing units across India. We are based in Mumbai, Maharashtra, and dispatch orders nationally through reliable logistics partners.
How do I know which dye to use for my fabric? +
The Dye Category Guide on this page gives a clear overview. As a rule of thumb: use VAT dyes for cotton requiring high fastness; Naphthol dyes for bold shades on cotton; Reactive dyes for vibrant colours on cotton, viscose, or silk. When in doubt, call us — Chirag K Shah can advise based on your specific fabric and shade requirements.
What does "M.F" mean in some product names? +
"M.F" stands for Machine Formulated — these are standardised, pre-blended formulations designed for consistency in automated or large-scale dyeing processes. They ensure even shade distribution across the batch with minimal manual adjustment.
